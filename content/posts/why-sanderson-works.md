## Hard Magic and Software Systems

As someone who builds software and reads a lot of fantasy, I've noticed something: Brandon Sanderson's magic systems feel like well-designed APIs.

His "First Law of Magic" is basically good software design: *An author's ability to solve conflict with magic is directly proportional to how well the reader understands said magic.*

## Consistent Rules Create Emergent Complexity

In the Mistborn series, Allomancy has clear, consistent rules:
- Burn metals to gain powers
- Each metal does one specific thing
- Powers combine in predictable ways

Sound familiar? It's compositional design. Simple primitives that combine to create complex behaviors.

This is exactly how good software systems work. Small, well-defined components that compose cleanly.

## The Payoff: Earned Victories

When magic (or software) has consistent rules, the victories feel earned. You can reason about what's possible. The hero doesn't win because of a deus ex machina - they win because they understood the system better than their opponent.

This is why I love both Sanderson's books and well-designed systems. They reward understanding.

## Currently Reading

I'm working through Terry Pratchett's Discworld now, which takes the opposite approach - soft magic, satirical, chaotic. It's a nice contrast and equally brilliant in its own way.

If you're into fantasy and haven't read Sanderson or Pratchett, start with *The Final Empire* (Mistborn) or *Guards! Guards!* (Discworld).
